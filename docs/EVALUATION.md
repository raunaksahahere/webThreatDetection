# Evaluation: URL rules on real domain lists (false positives and detection)

Generated by `scripts/eval_url_rules.py` on 2026-11-08 → `docs/evaluation-url-rules.json` (every flagged domain is listed there). Nothing was fetched: domains were only parsed.

**Scope: this measures one layer, the URL rules, with no page fetch, TLS, DNS/RDAP or threat-intelligence lookup.** In the full system the CERT Polska feed would mark every domain in the phishing sample Confirmed Malicious. That is why the feed was switched off here, and why these figures are not the system's detection rate.

## Data
- **Benign proxy:** Tranco list [94P72](https://tranco-list.eu/list/94P72) (created 2026-11-07). Popular sites are a proxy for benign, not a guarantee.
  - *Top 1–1,000*, with the popular-domain exception **disabled**, because these domains are inside the exception list. This measures the rules themselves.
  - *Held-out ranks 20,001–21,000*, in the shipped configuration. None of these are in the exception list.
- **Phishing:** CERT Polska Warning List, local snapshot (about 125k domains; its focus is threats to users in Poland).
  - A *development* sample of 1,000 (seed 7). Brand-list additions and the new rule were informed by looking at it.
  - A *held-out* sample of 1,000 (seed 99), disjoint from the development sample. This is the number to quote.

## Results

| Set | n | Flagged (Suspicious or worse) | High Risk | Rules that fired |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benign: Tranco top 1,000 (exception off) | 1000 | **7 (0.7%)** | 4 | typosquat ×7 |
| Benign: Tranco ranks 20,001–21,000 (held out) | 1000 | **4 (0.4%)** | 2 | typosquat ×2, wrong suffix ×1, brand in domain ×1 |
| Phishing: CERT Polska development sample | 1000 | 115 (11.5%) | 24 | brand in subdomain ×102, risky TLD ×91, fake-TLD label ×34 |
| Phishing: CERT Polska held-out sample | 1000 | **110 (11.0%)** | 28 | brand in subdomain ×91, risky TLD ×89, fake-TLD label ×41 |

The development and held-out phishing results are close (11.5% vs 11.0%), which suggests the changes generalise rather than fit the sample. "Rules that fired" lists each rule's top counts. One domain can trigger several rules.

### Remaining false positives (all listed, none hidden)
- Top 1,000 (exception off): mail.ru, alicdn.com, behance.net, telegraph.co.uk, hicloud.com, teads.tv, youku.com. These are all edit-distance look-alikes of protected names; in the shipped configuration the popular-domain exception covers them.
- Held out: vinted.lu, lmcdn.ru, mail.bg, olx-st.com. These were deliberately **not** tuned away after being seen, so the held-out figure stays honest.

## Before and after (same top-1,000 benign list, development phishing sample)

| | Benign top 1,000 flagged | Phishing (dev) flagged |
|---|---|---|
| Before (start of 2026-11-08 run) | 34 (3.4%) | 2 (0.2%) |
| After | 7 (0.7%) | 115 (11.5%) |

What changed:
1. The official-domain list was missing country, CDN and infrastructure domains for large brands (google.de, amazon.co.jp, mzstatic.com, whatsapp.net…). Brand-owned top-level domains are now recognised, so any `*.microsoft`, `*.google` or `*.sbi` domain counts as official.
2. A documented exception skips look-alike rules for the Tranco top-10k registrable domains (`backend/wtd/data/popular_domains.txt`). It is reported in the findings as `url.popular_domain`. Popularity is not proof of safety, and every other check still runs.
3. One- and two-letter official names (t.me, g.co) no longer make every `t.*` or `g.*` domain a "wrong suffix" fake.
4. New rule `url.fake_tld_label`: hosts built to read as another domain, e.g. `alebilet.pl-otsochodzi.shop` reads as `alebilet.pl`.
5. Brands that are among the most impersonated in this region were added: Allegro, OLX, InPost, DPD, PKO BP, mBank and Vinted, plus Booking.com, Telegram and Steam.

## What this does and does not show
- About 89% of real phishing domains look like nothing in particular from the URL alone. Lexical rules are a weak first layer. The system relies on threat feeds, TLS trust, page evidence (credential forms posting elsewhere, brand assets), domain age and message context. This is the reason those layers exist.
- A 0.4% URL-layer false-positive rate on unseen popular sites does not cover messages or page checks. Those false-positive controls are covered by the documented cases in `TESTING.md`: benign urgent messages, genuine OTP notices and official bank sites.
- Neither list is ground truth. Tranco can contain abusive domains, and CERT Polska entries can be cleaned up or expire.
